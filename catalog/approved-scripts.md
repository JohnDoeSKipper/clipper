## great-wave (paintings, approved 2026-11-22)

Brand new, this print cost a little more than two bowls of noodles.
Hokusai made it around 1831, aged about seventy, for a series of views of Mount Fuji.
But where is Fuji? Look below the claws of foam: that small triangle is Japan's tallest mountain.
Those boats are express barges racing fresh fish to Edo, and the wave towers about ten metres over them.
Its selling point was a new imported pigment, Prussian blue.
Printers pressed it twice where the wave is deepest, to give the water depth and movement.
It was never meant to be rare: up to eight thousand copies were printed, until the woodblocks wore out.
In 2023, one early copy sold for 2.76 million dollars.
Yet Hokusai thought he had barely started.
A few years later he wrote that nothing he drew before seventy was worth noticing.
At a hundred and ten, he promised, every dot and every line would be alive.
He died at eighty-nine, asking heaven for five more years to become a real artist.
